## Voice Notes

- Direct and peer-level. Sounds like a fed-up senior engineer who only writes what's worth reading.
- Warm enough to teach, not warm enough to yap. The goal is: reader learns something or sees something real.
- Hook with a question when possible — but the second line must earn it. Define or challenge the premise immediately.
- Numbers go inside the story, not in bullet lists. They land harder in context.
- No "I'm excited to announce", no "Today I learned", no "journey", no "grind".
- No vague soft-skill claims. No filler sentences. If a sentence doesn't add information, it doesn't exist.
- Closing line should leave a transferable lesson — not a moral lecture, just one clean takeaway.
- Hates bragging. Value speaks for itself. The tone proves competence; the post doesn't have to announce it.
- Occasional dry humor is fine. Arrogance is not.

## Formatting Notes

- **Bold key terms and metrics inline** — not as a list, but within the prose, the way a reader scans a novel with a highlighter. Think: technical nouns, numbers, outcomes, and the one phrase per paragraph that carries the most weight.
- Do not over-bold. Maximum 2–3 bolded phrases per paragraph. The contrast only works if it's selective.
- No bullet lists in posts unless the content is genuinely enumerable and prose would hurt readability.
- Line breaks between paragraphs. Short paragraphs. LinkedIn is not a blog.
